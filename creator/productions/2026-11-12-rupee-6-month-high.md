**Sound pass:** voice enhance at medium · music (M03 cinematic, from the Edits library) at 10–15% under voice, 25% in the gaps · **full silence 0.5s at 0:18** · SFX: thud, whoosh ×3, hit, pop, ticks, whomp, clicks. Each SFX quieter than the voice.

**How-to: the 3 hardest moves**
1. **Line chart (E-INFO-01):** in Canva, make a 1080×1920 design with a line chart (Jun ₹94.68 → Nov ~₹93.9; the y-axis should *not* start at 0 so the move is visible, and label it clearly) → Animate → Download as MP4 → in Edits, add it as a clip at 0:03.
2. **Counter "₹0 → ₹78 crore" (E-TEXT-09):** in Edits, add 5 text layers ("₹0", "₹20 cr", "₹45 cr", "₹65 cr", "₹78 crore"), each 3–4 frames long, back to back. Make the last one 1.5s with a scale pop. (CapCut alternative: a counter text template.)
3. **Headline slam (E-NEWS-01):** take a screenshot of the headline only (crop it, keep the publication name visible) → add it as a clip → keyframe scale at 130% (frame 0) and 100% (frame 4) → add the shake effect for 6 frames → overlay a red ✖ sticker at 0.6s.

**Export:** 1080×1920, 30fps, highest quality. Safe zone: nothing in the bottom 20% or right 15%. Save to the gallery for YouTube.

## 7. Look
- **Fonts:** EF4 typewriter for headlines, numbers and the flow graphic; EF3 clean sans for captions; the anchor ₹ ANGLE tag.
- **Captions (C02):** 2-line blocks, white, 10% black shadow, centre-upper third, keyword in accent; fix Hindi spellings by hand.
- **Text animation:** slide-up fade (E-TEXT-04) for normal text; scale pop only on the "78 crore" and "₹23,400" counters.
- **Grade G10 faded matte:** fade +15, contrast −10, saturation −10, warmth +3. Check that skin isn't grey.
- **Accent colour this post:** **teal #2A9D8F** (green-ish for "rupee up"); red only for the ✖ and ❌.
- **Cover (Canva, FP04 Archivo Black):** "RUPAYA MAZBOOT. FAAYDA?". Your face (eyebrows raised) on the right; a small line-chart arrow up; navy background; text in the centre 4:5 area.
  **Made:** https://canva.link/0bcf7flxhlitysn. Add your face cut-out (Canva "BG Remover") in the lower-middle, between the chart and the bottom 20%.
